i Business Structure The legal structure affects taxation, asset protection and Age Pension means testing. Active business assets under $750K may be exempt from Age Pension asset test if you work 20+ hours/week.
Example: Company structure provides asset protection but higher tax rates
i Business Active Asset Value Current market value of actively used business assets (equipment, stock, goodwill). Assets used in running the business may qualify for Age Pension exemption if you work 20+ hours weekly.
Example: $300K restaurant equipment and stock
i Business Ownership (%) Your percentage ownership in the business. This affects how much of the business value is attributed to you for Age Pension means testing and tax purposes.
Example: 50% ownership means 50% of business value counts toward your assets
i Years Business Held How long you've owned the business. This may affect Capital Gains Tax discount (50% if held >12 months) and small business CGT concessions for retirement.
Example: 15+ years may qualify for CGT retirement exemption
i Number of Investment Properties Total count of investment properties owned. Multiple properties increase rental income but also concentration risk and management complexity. All count toward Age Pension asset test.
Example: 3 properties = higher income but more risk than diversified portfolio
i Investment Property Cash Flow Whether rental income covers all property expenses (positive), breaks even (neutral), or requires additional funds (negative/negatively geared). Negative gearing provides tax benefits.
Example: Negative = $500/month out of pocket but tax deductions

Healthcare & Aged Care

i Aged Care Probability (%) Likelihood you'll need residential aged care. Australian statistics show ~70% of people will need aged care at some point. Consider family history, health, and gender (women have higher probability due to longer life expectancy).
Example: 68% means 68% chance of needing aged care costing ~$75k/year
i Expected Aged Care Start Age Average age when residential aged care begins. Australian average is around 85-88. Consider your health, family history, and lifestyle factors.
Example: Good health and family history suggests starting at 90
i Annual Aged Care Cost Total yearly cost for residential aged care including accommodation, care fees, and daily living expenses. Government subsidies reduce this, but you may still pay $40k-$100k+ annually.
Example: $76k/year includes accommodation payment + care fees

Economic & Asset Allocation

i Annual Inflation Rate (%) Expected long-term inflation rate. RBA targets 2-3% annually. Higher inflation erodes purchasing power, requiring larger retirement balances to maintain lifestyle.
Example: 3% inflation means $100 today = $74 purchasing power in 10 years
i Super Annual Growth (%) Expected annual return for your superannuation. Balanced super funds historically return 7-9% long-term. Growth options may achieve 9-11% but with higher volatility.
Example: 8% return doubles super balance roughly every 9 years
