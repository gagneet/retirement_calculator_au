Stress Test Results

Accumulation Phase

During the accumulation phase (from current age to retirement age), each year's balance is modelled as:

A(t+1) = (A(t) + C) × (1 + r)

Where A is the total balance, C is the annual contribution, and r is the annual return rate. Super and personal savings are tracked separately with their respective return assumptions.

Decumulation Phase

During retirement (from retirement age to planning horizon), each year's balance is modelled as:

D(t+1) = (D(t) − W_net) × (1 + r)

Where W_net is the net withdrawal after any Age Pension supplement, and r is the return rate. Withdrawals are drawn proportionally from super and savings balances. If inflation-adjusted withdrawals are enabled, W grows by CPI each year.

Age Pension Estimate

The model applies a simplified asset-test taper for a single homeowner (2024-25 rules):

  • Total assets ≤ $321,500 → full pension ($30,646/yr)
  • Total assets ≥ $714,500 → nil pension
  • Between thresholds → linear taper
  • Pension age: 67 years

This is a rough approximation. The actual Age Pension calculation uses both an assets test and an income test, and thresholds vary for couples and non-homeowners. Consult Services Australia for accurate eligibility.

Sustainable Income

The modelled annual income is derived from a growing annuity present-value formula, which calculates the maximum sustainable withdrawal level such that funds last to the planning horizon, given the blended return rate and inflation assumption.

Real vs Nominal

When "Display in real terms" is enabled, all projected balances and withdrawals are deflated by the cumulative CPI factor to express them in today's purchasing power (real dollars). Nominal figures are raw future amounts without adjustment.

Stress Tests

  • Sequence-of-returns: Return rates are set to −10% for the first 3 years of decumulation, then revert to the chosen assumption. This models the risk that poor early returns, combined with withdrawals, accelerate portfolio depletion.
  • Market crash: A one-time −25% balance shock is applied at the specified retirement year, modelling a severe correction event. Returns revert to assumptions thereafter.
